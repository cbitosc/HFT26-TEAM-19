Computer Vision, Edge AI & Hardware Security

AI and AR-powered hardware inspection and diagnostics

Engineers and hobbyists lose hours identifying tiny parts and cross-checking datasheets by hand. VisionGuard looks at a photo of a board, labels the parts it can see, reads their print, and gives step-by-step checks. It needs no pre-collected dataset.

It says what the photo supports, and nothing more. Team 19, HFT26.

Detect and label key components with open-source vision

Qwen3-VL (open weights, run through Ollama) and OpenCV find chips, resistors, capacitors, connectors and headers with no training dataset. On the labelled board it read all four chip markings correctly.

      Rules the code enforces

      None of these depends on what the model says.

      Only a person confirms

      The model and the look-alike search can never set a part to confirmed. Only a person's own action can.

      Text must be visible

      Markings that look like loops, or that sit on parts the model called unreadable, are discarded.

      Loops are cut

      Chains of identical boxes and repeated boxes are removed and listed, so a stuck model cannot invent a row of parts.

      Blank areas stay blank

      A box over a flat patch of the photo, such as a white banner, is dropped by checking the pixels.

      Look-alikes are labelled

      Parts found by appearance say so, carry no marking, and stay unknown until someone checks them.

      Local only

      The server listens on this computer only and the photo never leaves it.

      Not built yet

      • Finding burns, bulging capacitors and corrosion
      • A reference library with pin diagrams
      • Guidance, session and report endpoints
      • Resuming an analysis after a page reload

      Known limits

      • Truth boxes are drawn by eye
      • Both held-out photos are 500 px wide
      • Rows of tiny identical parts are often skipped by the model, so a "Find more like this" button searches by appearance
      • Phone capture over USB is untested
